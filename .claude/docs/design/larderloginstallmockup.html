Install as an app

One row in Settings › Preferences that installs the PWA — a real prompt where a browser offers one, two written steps where none exists. It does not appear when the app is already running standalone, or in any browser with no way to install. There is no banner: the reasons are in the doc, and the discovery trade it costs is on board 5.

Static copy of the design canvas, 28 Aug 2026. Boards are drawn at their real sizes — 1440 desktop, 390 phone, 340 drawer — so wide boards scroll inside their frame rather than shrinking.

Motion
The panel drops in over 180ms — height and fade together, ease-out — and closes in 140ms, matching the applied chip's exit. The row above it does not move; the blocks below it reflow with the pane. Under prefers-reduced-motion both are instant.
Nothing animates on install. The prompt is the browser's and it arrives on top of everything.
Keyboard and screen readers
The pill is a real button with aria-expanded in the iOS case, labelling the panel by its micro-label. Escape closes the panel back to the pill; focus returns to it. The steps are an ordered list, and the share mark carries the text Share beside it rather than only in a title attribute — an icon nobody can name is not an instruction.
What it does not get
No toast on install: the app appearing on the home screen is the confirmation, and you have left the browser to see it. No confirm — nothing is destroyed. No Installed ✓ state, because no browser reliably tells the page it is already installed, and a badge that lies on iOS is worse than none.

Why Preferences and not a block of its own
Scope is in the label: Preferences are yours, Pantry settings are the household's. Installing is yours. It does not follow you between devices — but nothing in this pane ever claimed to, so the meta line says On this device. and the rule survives. A fourth block for one row is the thing the add/edit sheet already argued against.
Two inherited findings, flagged rather than fixed
Drawer meta on the raised fill. #9E8C74 reads 4.28:1 there — under 4.5 for text, though fine at 5.02 on the drawer gradient itself. These rows take #A5937A. It is the same shape as the faint text does not clear 4.5 finding in the add/edit doc, one surface over.

When the row appears

One condition, twice: a path exists, and you are not already in the app. Plus the discovery trade and the prompts considered and not drawn.

When the row appears
One surface now, and one condition: a path exists, and you are not already in the app
ContextThe rowWhy
Running as the installed app — The rule the whole thing is built around. display-mode: standalone, or navigator.standalone on iOS. Nothing offers to install what is already open.
Chromium — desktop or mobile
beforeinstallprompt captured
 Install The one case where a real prompt exists. The pill fires the saved event; the browser draws the rest.
iOS Safari Show me No prompt exists, so the steps are the control. This is most of the phone audience, and it is now the only place they can find out at all.
macOS Safari 17+
Add to Dock, from the File menu
 Open A third steps variant, or hide the row and accept that Mac Safari users never learn. Not drawn.
Any browser with no install path — A control that can only disappoint — the rule the sort trigger and the cart trigger already follow.
Signed out
marketing page, sign-in, invite landing
 n/a There is no drawer out there. You install a pantry you have.
A household with no items yet Install Nothing to wait for. Settings is somewhere you went on purpose, so it has no reason to hold anything back.
Viewer role Install It survives the read-only cut untouched, like Appearance. Installing is a fact about your browser, not a power over the household.
The cost of dropping the banner, stated plainly
Nobody opens Settings to see what is in it. With the banner gone, installing is reachable only by someone who already suspects it is possible — which, on iOS, is the person who least needs telling. This design does not solve discovery; it declines to. That is a defensible trade — the banner cost ~125px at the top of a 390 screen and asked for something nobody had asked for — but it should be a decision on the record rather than a gap noticed later.
What was considered and not drawn
A marker on the Settings tab that clears once seen — the drawer has no vocabulary for one, and the mobile menu button's crimson badge is already spoken for by the filter count. A line in the account menu — wrong scope, that menu is about who you are. A mention on the marketing page — it would be describing a browser feature to someone who has not signed up.
Open
Installed, but viewing in a browser. No reliable signal outside getInstalledRelatedApps() on Android, so the row keeps offering something already done. Harmless in Settings, where it would have been a nuisance in a banner — one small argument for this version.
Where Share is. On iPhone it is the bar at the bottom, on iPad the toolbar at the top. Step 1 names the button and not its place, which is one clause short on the platform that needs it most.
Every colour is from the Larder Log token tables. Two move, and both are recorded as findings: drawer meta on the raised fill goes to #A5937A (#9E8C74 reads 4.28:1 there), and the inline composer's panel hairline goes to #6E5F4B (#3B3126 reads 1.10:1 — no edge at all).
