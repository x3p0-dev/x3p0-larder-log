The flow

SETTLED, 31 Aug — the sheet is not where this lives. The primary grows a chevron and the chevron holds the routes. Day one keeps its spelled-out ghost, because the empty state has the room and nothing else to compete with. What that buys: the Add sheet goes back to being about one item. No ghost row, no segmented header, no footer link — three rounds of trying to fit "many" into a surface built for "one", all now on the Explorations page. The sheet gains one thing instead, and it is not about many: Save and add another. Board 4.
The split button is the first of its kind in this app, so it has to be specced rather than assumed — which is what board 3 is. The part still open is 390: a 34px chevron cell is under the 44px floor every other mobile control holds. Growing it to 44 costs search another 10px, and search at 240 on a phone is its own problem.
The parse reads a name, a count, and a size when a unit follows a number. It never guesses a location, store or type — a paste can't know them.
One review table, two sources. Nothing is written until Add. Duplicates arrive unchecked and amber — the "hold on" rung, not crimson: nothing is being destroyed. "Set for all" is the part that decides whether this works. Bulk entry that leaves you assigning three chips per row two hundred times has not solved the wall it was built for.
The checklist feeds the same table rather than committing directly, and that is the whole reason it is drawn this way. Adding 31 items straight from a tick means 31 items at 0 on hand — which, by the shopping list's own rule, is 31 rows on your list on day one. Routing through review, where counts default to 1, avoids it. It is also the phone-shaped path: nobody pastes 200 lines on a phone, but ticking boxes one-handed is exactly what a phone is for.

Mobile

Three things the phone decides that the desktop boards left open. The paste box is a BOTTOM SHEET here, not the centred confirm shell. A confirm is a question and centres away from the thumb; this is an entry surface with a keyboard about to cover half the screen, so it takes the Add sheet's shape. The review row STACKS two-deep — which is the exact shape that made sheet option H unworkable at 480. At 358 there is no other shape, so what was a compromise on desktop is simply the row here. And the menu order should probably FLIP at this width: pasting two hundred lines one-handed is not a thing anyone does, and ticking boxes is.
