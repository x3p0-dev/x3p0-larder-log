Why the pane
Delete account sits inside the account's own card, under a hairline — exactly where Leave household sits inside the Household card. That parallel is the argument, not a coincidence: this is leaving, at the scale of every household at once.
It answers the ceiling rather than meeting it. Announcements put the menu at four rows and named the fifth as the point where the construction stops being a menu — and named account deletion as the thing that would bring the question back. It came back, and the answer is the pane.
Export needed the same home. Two account-scoped rows arrived in one week. A menu absorbs one at a push; a pane absorbs both without being asked. See board 5.
The account row stays visible beneath it, as it does beneath Members and Announcements — so the door you came through is on screen the whole time you are in here.
The cost, and it is one thing
The display name moves out of the menu. Settings tab put it there on purpose — no modal, no profile screen; it is where you already were — and the pencil cannot live in two places. The idiom survives intact: a read-only row that flips in place, no modal, Escape cancels, no toast. What changes is the surface it happens on, one push further in.

Four decisions on this screen
Amber, not crimson. This screen is the blocked dialog turned into a choice, so it keeps the blocked dialog's disc. Nothing is destroyed by looking at it — amber is “hold on”, and this is the last screen where that is still true.
The primary is Continue, and it is the one primary in the app that doesn't name a destructive verb. Every other confirm says Revoke invite, Leave household, Delete household. Pressing this one destroys nothing; the verb is on the next screen, where it belongs.
 
One trigger, not two chips. Transfer and delete look like a pair until you notice that transfer needs a name — so it is one question with several answers, which is a menu. It takes the role trigger's shape and the sort menu's cream construction, per the console's rule for a menu opening on a card. See board 3.
Nothing to decide is a group, not the console's tail line. A sole-member household is destroyed and two more are merely left — three facts, and a single sentence flattens the first into the others. The row that loses 34 items says so in the out text colour, on a screen where nothing is pressable to fix it.

The menu, and one extension to it
Cream, because it opens on a card. The role menu is dark in the drawer and cream in the console, and the existing rule already picks: this one opens on the confirm shell, so it takes the sort menu's construction — 6px padding, radius-9 rows, hover fill and nothing else.
No row is marked as current, because there is no current value. The sort menu and the role menu both check the value you are on; a transfer has no incumbent — every row is a thing that has not happened yet. So the check comes off, and hover is the only state the rows have.
It carries a micro-label header, which no menu in the app has. Two kinds of row — three people and one destruction — and without the label the delete row reads as a fourth person. The hairline alone was not enough on the board.
Every member is offered, whatever their role. Receiving it promotes them, which the confirm says out loud. Filtering to existing owners would hide the only candidate in a household that has exactly one.

Promote is not transfer
Setting someone to Owner adds an owner. Transfer ownership hands it over: they become Owner and you become an Editor. The second half is the part with no control today — your own row has no trigger, so there is nowhere to step yourself back from.
Both rows in one menu is the risk. The label carries it: Owner is a role you set, Transfer ownership is a thing you do. If that turns out to be too fine a distinction, transfer moves to the Household card beside Leave household, where the verbs are.
It is not crimson. Nothing is destroyed, and ghost-plus-crimson-text is how this app offers destruction. It is an ordinary row in ordinary drawer body, between the roles and the removal.
The disc rule, generalised
The confirm's disc is crimson, though nothing is destroyed. Blocked takes amber because it is a precondition; a confirm takes crimson because it is final. Losing ownership passes the second test and fails the first, so the ramp is picked by finality rather than by data loss — which is the existing two users generalised, not a new rule.
Nobody tells Sarah. There is no person-to-person channel in the app: announcements are app-to-person, toasts are for what you did, and email is a pipe future-ideas has priced and nobody has built. She finds out in Members. Recorded as a gap rather than solved — and it is sharper now that a transfer can arrive because someone deleted their account and left.

There is no hold, and that is a decision
Deleting is immediate. The industry default is a thirty-day grace period, and it was drawn: three cards, a countdown, a Keep my account primary. It is gone. If someone wants to delete their account, that is their decision — the app does not hold onto people who have said no.
The design cost of the hold was never the three cards; it was that a held account is already out of its households, so the grace period gives you back an account with nothing in it. A month of ambiguity for a consolation prize is the worse trade, and the typed confirmation is where the deliberation belongs.
The console has to catch up. Awaiting deletion in Needs attention, and Automatic as the actor on the row that deletes an account after its hold, both describe a state that no longer exists. See the deltas.
What you type, and the body being a list
The display name, not the email. A typed confirmation buys a beat of deliberation, not authentication — it is a rhythm-breaker, and nobody has ever been stopped by one they could paste. So it takes the name a person thinks of as theirs, and the one the rest of the household has been seeing. The email would be more precise about which account and worse at the only job the field has.
The body is a list, which is new. Every other confirm's body is two lines at most, because it names one thing that is lost. This one names five households in three fates, and a sentence that tried would be the worst paragraph in the app. The recap is the pre-flight's own rows, read-only, with the triggers gone.
No toast. There is no app left to show one in — the card above is the confirmation, and it is the fifth settled case in States an SPA hits constantly.

One file, no picker. A format choice is a question nobody has an opinion about. The terms come out as their names, not their ids, so the file is readable on its own — and an empty store is an empty cell, exactly as NO STORE is a real state.
These are the columns Paste your list should read. bulk-entry.md parses a name, a count and a size and deliberately never guesses a location, store or type. Export is the shape an import could accept — moving a pantry between households, which nothing today can do.

It appears only on a row set to delete, which is the only moment in the app where a pantry is about to stop existing and someone is looking straight at it. A row set to transfer doesn't get it — the household keeps its own copy and its own export row.
Why it splits
Your data is an obligation; the pantry is the feature. Wherever deletion is required by law it arrives with a right to a copy — and the copy the law means is those four fields, which nobody wants. The copy people actually want is the 47 rows that belong to a household, and it survives your deletion because it was never yours.
Putting both behind one Export row would have been the natural move and it would have made the same mistake the deletion copy is at pains to avoid: implying the pantry goes with you.
Neither is a backup, because nothing imports one back. Saying so is cheap and the alternative is someone deleting a household they thought they had saved.

What changes at 390, and what doesn't
The row keeps its shape. Every other list in the app restacks at this width; this one doesn't have to, because the trigger's label is one short word. It grows 30 → 44px and the row grows with it, and that is the whole adaptation.
It stays centred rather than becoming a bottom sheet. A confirm is a question, and centring keeps it out of the thumb zone the Add sheet owns — a question with a list in it is still a question. bulk-entry.md went the other way for Paste your list because that one is an entry surface with a keyboard coming; nothing here types until the last screen.
Two things this board can't answer
Someone in eleven households. Five households are drawn; eleven scroll, and a dialog that scrolls has to decide whether its footer is pinned. Not drawn, and the first thing to draw if this ships.
The typed confirm with a keyboard up. It is the taller of the two dialogs and its field sits near the bottom, above the footer pair; the keyboard takes about 340px of the screen. That is the one place in this flow where the centred choice may not hold, and it is not drawn.
