Garden and Kitchen modes

Three structural options for how grown and home-made items reach a list, plus where a store’s kind is set and what it changes on an item. All six boards from the canvas, at 1440, light theme.

Nothing here is settled design. The tokens, type ramp and control sizes are lifted from claude/ui-designs.md and claude/add-edit-item.md; the twenty-item sample set is extended to thirty so the new bands have something real to draw.

Settled

OPTION A + C — ONE LIST, THREE BANDS, AND A SEGMENT

The rule this rests on: a source carries a KIND — Shop, Grow, Make. Nothing else in the model changes. The Garden is a source you pick from; The Kitchen is a source you cook from.

The list opens on ALL, which is A's banded screen — so the adjacency that decided A is what you see first: "you're out of stock, and the carrots for it are on the Publix card two bands up." The segment then buys C's focused views for the moments the activities separate — at the shop, in the garden, at the stove.

It costs nothing for a household with no grow or make source: no second kind, no segment, no bands.

The group is named for what it holds — Store with one kind, Source with more. Drawn light-theme only; the dark counterparts are a hex-for-hex map away.

The Harvest and Make bands each hold one card in a two-column grid, and the card stays 642px rather than stretching across the column.

That is auto-fill doing exactly what Shopping list says it is there for: "with one store card left after a filter, auto-fit would stretch it across the whole screen." The bands got that behaviour for free.

OPEN, AND DELIBERATELY NOT DECIDED HERE

1. "Source" is the working word. Where from is the plainer alternative and reads closer to this app's voice, at the cost of being two words in a one-word slot. Origin and Supply were both colder than anything else in the interface.

2. What the trigger is called on the way in. "Shopping list" stops being literally true; "To get" is honest but renames the headline feature and the marketing page with it. The segment took some of the pressure off, not all of it.

3. Dropping the count from the editing row loses something real. Destructive actions added it so the outcome of pressing the trash was predictable before you reached for it; now the blocked dialog is the only place you learn that a term is in use. The kind glyph is what took the space.

4. Track "X" as a new item, in the ingredient picker, writes to the pantry from inside a sheet about a different item. It lands with 0 on hand, which puts it straight onto the shopping list. Drawn, not settled.

5. What checking a Harvest or Make row does. Picking and cooking are both restocks — a write — which is the reserved half of the trip bar, and the reason Restock still blocks this.

6. Season is per item here. It could belong to the grow source instead.

7. Whether wanting to make something should pull its ingredients onto the buy list. Stock needs 3 carrots and you have 1: the list says nothing, because it is a view of counts against low-at and nothing else. Demand-driven restocking is a real feature and a real change to that rule.

8. Knock-ons of the rename, not drawn: the rail's storefront glyph means "shop", and the marketing page's band says Location, Store, Type.

Where this goes, and what lost

WHERE THIS GOES, AND WHAT LOST

The first board is not an exploration of the past — it is the next question. Recipes and gardening are the destination, so the test is no longer "does this belong in a pantry app" but "does today's design leave room". Two models for turning a make item into a recipe card, drawn side by side because the choice is not obvious.

Below them, the two structures that lost. B gave each activity its own mode and lost outright: row 2's composition would depend on the household. C is a record rather than a proposal — its segment was adopted onto the settled run list, but with an All tab in front of it.

Boards are shown at their canvas size and scaled to fit the window — widen the browser for a closer look, or print to PDF. Light theme only, per the Settings canvas precedent: the dark counterparts are a hex-for-hex map away.
