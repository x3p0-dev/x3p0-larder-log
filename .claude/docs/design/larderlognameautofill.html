The rules

What the menu does, before anyone presses anything
Settled 31 Aug: pressing a row takes the name, the three term chips and the size — never the counts. See Picking.
Opening and matching
Two characters, word prefixes, six rows, no scroll
It opens at two characters, never on focus. An empty name field offering six common groceries is the app guessing at what you came to do.
A match is a prefix of any word, not a substring anywhere — be finds Ground Beef and Black Beans, eef finds nothing. The matched characters go to 700, which is the whole explanation of the rule and costs no space.
Six rows, at most three per group, and it never scrolls. A suggestion list you scroll is one you should have typed another letter into. The unit menu scrolls because fifteen units are a fixed set you are choosing from; this is a guess you can improve.
Absence, and the groups
Nothing matches, so nothing opens
There is no “No matches” row. A menu that opens to report an absence covers the sheet to tell you what the empty list already said. Typing a name nothing knows is the ordinary case, not an error.
Groups get micro-labels, where the sort and unit menus get bare hairlines. Those menus group six sorts and fifteen units — variants of one thing. These two are different kinds of answer, and which kind a row is changes what pressing it does. A hairline cannot say that.
Order is fixed: pantry, then catalog. The most consequential match leads.
Keyboard, screen readers, and one thing cut
The field becomes a combobox
↓ ↑ move the highlight, Enter takes it, Escape closes the menu and keeps what you typed — joining the unit menu and the composer ahead of the sheet in Escape's order. Tab commits the typed text, not the highlight.
combobox with aria-autocomplete="list" over a listbox — the same shape the unit trigger already takes, two controls apart on one row.
Terms were cut from this menu on 31 Aug. Baking the type and Baking Soda the item collided in a field labelled Item, and setting a chip from the name field was a second subject in one control. Search gives terms a proper home, in a group whose whole job is to apply one — so two groups here, three labels across the two menus.

Picking

What pressing a row does. Settled 31 Aug.

Settled 31 Aug. Picking a row that matches something you already have brings its name, its Location / Store / Type chips and its size across. On hand and low at never move. The watch-out is on the board: this makes a duplicate one tap away, and nothing in the design catches it. If that bites, C on the Explorations page is the answer that was drawn for it.

The case for
Restocking is the commonest reason to add an item, and a restock is a near-clone. One press does the whole sheet.
The watch-out
It writes four sections you are not looking at — on a 390 sheet, three of them are below the fold. And it makes the duplicate easier: the fastest path to a second Ground Beef is now one tap. Nothing in this design catches that; it is the first thing to watch on a real household.

Search

The top-bar search dropdown — item rows jump, term rows filter.

The search menu answers four questions the doc has had open since the build was read off a screenshot: what search matches, whether it reaches the term lists, its focus state, and whether it is debounced. It is the name field's menu, re-pointed: same 440 width, same rows, same group headings. What differs is one rule — a chevron means the row leaves the screen you are on — and only search has rows like that. Still unsettled: with a query on, the status pills read 0 in stock · 2 running low · 0 out. Two pills at zero is the case the doc flags as "a control that can only disappoint", and nobody has looked at one on a real screen. On both desktop boards the menu covers that row, which is itself worth noticing.

What the search menu does

What the search menu does
Four of the standing questions under Gaps → the document has drifted from the build get an answer here. The two menus are one component: same 440 width, same 6px padding and 9px rows at 12px inset, same group headings — In your pantry · Common items · Terms — and the same word-prefix highlight.
The two verbs
A chevron means the row leaves this screen
An item row opens that item's Edit sheet. A term row applies the term; it then appears in the applied-filter row, where Clear filters can take it off again — no new component, and that bar was built for exactly this. It is not drawn on these boards because the state they show has no filter on yet.
One menu, two verbs, and the chevron is the whole of what has to be learned. It appears on the rows that navigate and nowhere else — the name field's menu has no such row, so it never carries one.
A term row does not close the menu. Terms are a set you work through, which is the rule the rail's quick filters already hold. An item row closes it, because it has taken you somewhere.
What it matches — closing an open question
Names, sizes, and term names. Never notes.
Typing pint finds your pints, which is what the size section asked for. Notes stay out: a row in the list for a reason invisible in the row is worse than a shorter list.
Matching is name-shaped, and that is why there are two groups. Typing co does not return Costco's six items — it returns the things called co-something, and offers Costco as a term beside them. Pressing that row is how you get the six.
Word prefixes, same as the name field: co finds Frozen Corn, orn finds nothing. Matched characters go to 700.
Size and absence
The menu is a shortcut, never the results
The grid behind it is already narrowed. Everything the menu could list is on screen underneath, so there is no see all row and no scroll. The cap is five items and three terms; the query drawn on these boards only turns up two of each.
Nothing matches, so nothing opens. The grid carries it instead, in the item-grid empty state's own form — Playfair italic over a meta line and one ghost control — not the shopping list's status disc. A disc would be the status ramp saying something about stock, and this is about a query.
Width, focus, keys
440, left-aligned — the field is a banner, not a list
440 — one column of the grid, and the same width as the name field's menu. Left-aligned to the field. Letting it take the field's real width (~1221 at 1372) would put a two-word name in an acre of nothing; at 440 it covers exactly one card and never clips a neighbour mid-word.
The focus state, also open until now: border to line strong plus the crimson halo every other field on the app takes. Nothing new.
Escape closes the menu and keeps the query; a second Escape clears the field — the same two steps the field's own × collapses into one. Not debounced at twenty items; that becomes a question at a few hundred, not before.

The case for
It is the only one that stops the duplicate rather than describing it. In a shared list two people add the same thing constantly, and this is where it can be caught.
The cost
One menu, two verbs — most rows complete a word, these navigate away. And it throws away what you typed to open a different sheet, which is the one thing a suggestion menu is not supposed to do.
The seam
On the Edit sheet it is nonsense — a signpost to where you already are. The rule that saves it is the one all three need anyway: the item you are editing never appears in its own menu.
